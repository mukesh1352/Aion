Brahmi is one of the most important ancient writing systems of South Asia.

It was used for inscriptions and other written material and later developed into or contributed to numerous writing systems across the Indian subcontinent.

## Early Evidence

Some proposed early examples of Brahmi remain debated.

The securely dated and extensive use of Brahmi becomes especially visible in inscriptions from the Mauryan period.

Ashokan inscriptions provide some of the most important early evidence for the script.

## Ashokan Inscriptions

During the reign of Ashoka in the third century BCE, inscriptions were placed across large parts of the Mauryan Empire.

Most of these inscriptions in the Indian heartland were written in Brahmi.

They recorded royal messages concerning administration, moral conduct, and Dhamma.

## Writing Direction

Brahmi was generally written from left to right.

This distinguishes it from Kharosthi, another important ancient script of the northwest, which was normally written from right to left.

## Development

Brahmi did not remain a single unchanged script.

Different regional forms developed over time.

Later scripts of South Asia can be traced through historical stages to Brahmi or related developments.

## Decipherment

The systematic decipherment of Brahmi in the nineteenth century was a major development in the study of ancient Indian history.

It allowed scholars to read many inscriptions that had previously been inaccessible.

## Historical Importance

Brahmi inscriptions provide direct evidence for language, political authority, religious activity, names, titles, and geography in ancient South Asia.

They are therefore among the most important primary sources for reconstructing early Indian history.
