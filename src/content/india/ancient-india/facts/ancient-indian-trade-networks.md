Ancient South Asia was connected by networks of trade that operated across land, rivers, and seas.

These networks linked agricultural regions, cities, ports, political centers, and communities across large distances.

## Inland Routes

Overland routes connected cities across northern India and the northwest.

Merchants transported goods between agricultural regions, urban centers, and frontier zones.

## River Networks

The Ganges and its tributaries provided important routes for movement.

River transport could be particularly valuable for moving agricultural products and other heavy goods.

## Northwestern Connections

The northwest connected South Asia with Central Asia, Iran, and regions farther west.

Taxila was one of the important urban centers situated within these wider networks.

## Maritime Trade

Ports along the western and eastern coasts connected the subcontinent with the Arabian Sea, Persian Gulf, and Bay of Bengal.

Maritime networks developed over long periods and linked Indian merchants and coastal communities with other parts of the Indian Ocean world.

## Goods

Trade involved many different products.

These included textiles, spices, metals, beads, precious stones, agricultural products, and other manufactured or natural goods.

The exact commodities varied by region and period.

## Urban Centers

Growing cities benefited from their position within trade networks.

Markets and specialized crafts developed alongside increasing urban populations.

## Political Connections

Political states benefited from trade through taxation, control of routes, and access to resources.

The expansion of states such as Magadha and later the Mauryan Empire therefore occurred within a wider economic landscape.

## Historical Significance

Trade networks helped connect otherwise distant communities.

They contributed to the movement of goods, people, ideas, technologies, and religious traditions across ancient South Asia and beyond.
