A major phase of urban development took place in northern India during the first millennium BCE.

Archaeologists and historians often describe this period as the period of the "Second Urbanization," distinguishing it from the earlier cities of the Indus civilization.

## Growth of Settlements

New and expanding settlements appeared across the Gangetic plain and surrounding regions.

Cities such as Rajgir, Vaishali, Kaushambi, and later Pataliputra became important political and economic centers.

## Agriculture

Agricultural expansion supported larger populations.

The fertile plains of the Ganges and its tributaries provided productive agricultural environments.

The increasing use of iron tools may have contributed to the expansion of cultivation in some regions, although urbanization had multiple causes.

## Craft Production

Urban centers supported specialized crafts.

Pottery, metalworking, bead production, textiles, and other activities developed alongside expanding populations and markets.

## Trade

Growing cities participated in regional trade networks.

Rivers, roads, and overland routes connected settlements with agricultural hinterlands and distant regions.

## Political Organization

The growth of cities was closely connected with the rise of territorial states.

The Mahajanapadas competed for territory and resources, while states such as Magadha developed increasingly centralized political structures.

## Religious Communities

Urban environments also became important centers for religious movements.

Buddhist and Jain communities developed within the same broader social and economic environment.

## Archaeological Evidence

Archaeologists study settlement layers, fortifications, pottery, coins, craft remains, and other material evidence to reconstruct early historic urban life.

## Historical Significance

Early urbanization transformed the political and economic landscape of northern India.

It created the cities, markets, administrative centers, and social environments in which larger states and religious communities could develop.
