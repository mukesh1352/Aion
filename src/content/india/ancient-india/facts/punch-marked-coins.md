Punch-marked coins are among the most important forms of early coinage found in the Indian subcontinent.

They were generally made from silver and were produced by striking individual symbols onto pieces of metal.

## Appearance

Unlike modern coins, punch-marked coins were often irregular in shape.

Several symbols could be impressed onto the surface using separate punches.

The symbols vary between coin series and regions.

## Dating

Punch-marked coinage is generally associated with the first millennium BCE.

Some examples are connected with the period of the Mahajanapadas and later with the Mauryan period.

Exact dates vary between individual coin groups.

## Economic Activity

The existence and circulation of coinage provide evidence for developing systems of exchange and monetized economic activity.

Coins could facilitate transactions beyond direct barter, particularly in growing urban and commercial environments.

## Symbols

Punch-marked coins contain a variety of symbols.

These may include animals, plants, geometric forms, celestial symbols, and other motifs.

The precise meaning of many individual symbols remains uncertain.

## Regional Circulation

Different regional traditions of punch-marked coinage existed across the subcontinent.

Their distribution helps archaeologists study trade, political boundaries, and economic connections.

## Mauryan Period

Punch-marked coins continued to circulate during the Mauryan period.

Their widespread presence is consistent with the existence of substantial commercial and administrative networks.

## Historical Significance

Punch-marked coins provide material evidence for the development of markets, taxation, trade, and monetary practices in ancient South Asia.

They are particularly valuable because they complement literary and archaeological evidence from early urban centers.
