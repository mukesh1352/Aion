The story of Angulimala is one of the best-known narratives in Buddhist literature.

It presents a violent man whose encounter with the Buddha leads to a transformation from destructive behavior to religious discipline.

## Angulimala

Angulimala is portrayed in Buddhist texts as a dangerous figure who lived outside ordinary social order.

The story associates him with killing travelers and collecting their fingers.

The name Angulimala is traditionally interpreted in connection with a garland of fingers.

## The Encounter

According to the narrative, the Buddha deliberately traveled toward the region where Angulimala lived.

Angulimala attempted to pursue him.

Although Angulimala was running, he could not catch the Buddha.

## The Statement

Angulimala eventually confronted the Buddha and demanded that he stop.

The Buddha replied that he had already stopped, meaning that he had ceased harming living beings, while Angulimala had not.

The statement caused Angulimala to reconsider his actions.

## Transformation

Angulimala abandoned his violent life and became a Buddhist monk.

The story then describes the consequences of his earlier actions and the difficulties he faced even after changing his life.

## Moral Meaning

The narrative emphasizes transformation rather than denying the seriousness of past actions.

It presents the possibility of moral change while also acknowledging consequences.

## Historical Status

The Angulimala story belongs primarily to Buddhist literary and religious tradition.

It should not be treated as a securely documented biography of a historical individual.

## Significance

The story became an influential Buddhist teaching about violence, remorse, transformation, compassion, and the possibility of changing one's life.
