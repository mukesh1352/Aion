The Great Renunciation is one of the central stories in Buddhist accounts of the life of Siddhartha Gautama.

It describes the moment when Siddhartha abandoned his former life and began the search that eventually led to his awakening.

## Life in the Palace

Buddhist traditions describe Siddhartha as having lived in a privileged environment before his renunciation.

The stories portray his family as seeking to protect him from the realities of suffering.

## The Four Sights

According to Buddhist tradition, Siddhartha eventually encountered an old person, a sick person, a dead person, and a wandering ascetic.

These encounters forced him to confront the realities of aging, illness, and death.

The ascetic represented the possibility of seeking a spiritual answer to these problems.

## Leaving Home

Siddhartha became determined to search for liberation.

The traditional narrative describes him leaving his household during the night and beginning a life as a wandering seeker.

## The Search

Siddhartha studied with teachers and practiced severe forms of asceticism.

He eventually rejected extreme austerity and developed a different approach to spiritual practice.

## From Renunciation to Enlightenment

The Great Renunciation is therefore not presented as an isolated event.

It begins the journey that Buddhist tradition connects with Siddhartha's eventual enlightenment at Bodh Gaya.

## Historical and Traditional Layers

The detailed narrative comes from Buddhist literary traditions developed and transmitted over time.

The precise circumstances of Siddhartha's departure are therefore difficult to establish historically.

## Significance

The story became a model of spiritual renunciation and remains one of the most influential narratives in Buddhist tradition.
