The story of Chandragupta Maurya and Chanakya is one of the best-known political narratives associated with the foundation of the Mauryan Empire.

It combines historical memory with later literary traditions.

## Chanakya

Chanakya is traditionally portrayed as a learned political strategist who became determined to challenge the Nanda rulers of Magadha.

He is also known as Kautilya and Vishnugupta in different traditions.

## Meeting Chandragupta

Later narratives describe Chanakya encountering the young Chandragupta and recognizing his political potential.

The stories differ in their details, and the historical circumstances of their relationship cannot be reconstructed with certainty.

## The Nanda Court

A recurring element of the tradition describes Chanakya becoming hostile toward the Nanda ruler.

The story then presents him as seeking a new leader capable of overthrowing the dynasty.

## Building Power

The traditional narrative describes Chanakya guiding Chandragupta through political and military struggles.

Together they are said to have built support and eventually defeated the Nandas.

## The Mauryan Empire

Chandragupta became the founder of the Mauryan dynasty.

Chanakya is traditionally remembered as an important adviser in the establishment of the new state.

## The Arthashastra

Chanakya is traditionally associated with the *Arthashastra*, a major Sanskrit work on politics, administration, economics, law, and statecraft.

The dating and authorship history of the text are complex, and the surviving work should not automatically be treated as a direct record of Chanakya's personal advice to Chandragupta.

## Historical Memory

The Chandragupta-Chanakya story has been retold in Sanskrit, Jain, Buddhist, and later literary traditions.

Some details are legendary, while the broader association between Chandragupta and a political adviser named Chanakya/Kautilya is deeply established in Indian historical memory.

## Significance

The story represents the transition from the Nanda dynasty to Mauryan rule.

It also became one of the enduring narratives of political strategy and state formation in Indian historical tradition.
