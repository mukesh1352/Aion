The relationship between King Bimbisara of Magadha and the Buddha occupies an important place in Buddhist tradition.

The story connects the political world of an expanding Magadhan kingdom with the religious movements developing in the eastern Gangetic region.

## Bimbisara

Bimbisara was an important ruler of the Haryanka dynasty of Magadha.

His reign is associated with the strengthening and expansion of Magadhan political power.

Rajgir was an important center of his kingdom.

## Meeting the Buddha

Buddhist traditions describe Bimbisara encountering Siddhartha Gautama before or around the beginning of the Buddha's teaching career.

The king is said to have recognized Siddhartha's spiritual potential.

## The Buddha's Teaching

After attaining enlightenment, the Buddha returned to regions of the eastern Gangetic plain and began teaching.

Bimbisara is traditionally described as becoming a supporter of the Buddha and his community.

## Veluvana

Buddhist tradition associates Bimbisara with the donation of Veluvana, or the Bamboo Grove, to the Buddha and his followers.

It became an important monastic residence in the Buddhist tradition.

## Royal Patronage

The story illustrates the relationship that developed between wandering religious teachers and political rulers.

Royal support could provide communities with land, protection, and access to important urban centers.

## Historical Evidence

Bimbisara is remembered in multiple traditions as an important ruler of Magadha.

The details of his personal relationship with the Buddha come primarily from Buddhist sources and should therefore be distinguished from independently documented political history.

## Significance

The Bimbisara-Buddha tradition helps explain how Buddhism became connected with the political and urban environment of the eastern Gangetic region.
