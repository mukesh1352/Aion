Pataliputra was one of the most important cities of ancient South Asia.

Located in the eastern Gangetic plain, it developed from a Magadhan settlement into the capital of one of the largest empires of the ancient Indian world.

## From Magadha to Empire

The city became increasingly important as Magadha expanded.

Its position near major rivers provided advantages for transportation, communication, agriculture, and military movement.

## Mauryan Capital

Under the Mauryas, Pataliputra became the principal imperial capital.

Chandragupta Maurya established his court there, and the city remained central during the reigns of Bindusara and Ashoka.

## Megasthenes

The Greek ambassador Megasthenes visited the Mauryan court.

His account, known as *Indica*, survives only in fragments preserved by later writers.

These fragments provide descriptions of Pataliputra and the Mauryan political system, although they must be interpreted carefully.

## Urban Development

Ancient descriptions portray Pataliputra as a large and organized urban center.

Archaeological discoveries have revealed wooden structures, defensive features, pottery, coins, and other remains associated with the ancient settlement.

## Rivers and Strategic Location

Pataliputra's location near the Ganges and other waterways contributed to its strategic importance.

River transport connected the city with other parts of the Gangetic plain and helped support its political and economic role.

## Historical Significance

Pataliputra became a symbol of Magadhan and Mauryan political power.

Its history demonstrates how river geography, agriculture, trade, and political centralization combined to produce major urban centers in ancient India.
