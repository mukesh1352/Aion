Rajgir, known in ancient sources as Rajagriha, was one of the earliest major centers of the kingdom of Magadha.

Located in a valley surrounded by hills, the city occupied a strategically defensible position in the eastern Gangetic region.

## Magadhan Capital

Rajagriha served as an important capital of Magadha before the political center shifted toward Pataliputra.

The city's natural geography provided protection and made it suitable for a fortified settlement.

## Bimbisara and Ajatashatru

Bimbisara and Ajatashatru, two important rulers of early Magadha, are strongly associated with Rajagriha in Buddhist traditions.

The city formed part of the political environment in which Magadha developed into a major regional power.

## Buddhist Traditions

Rajgir occupies an important place in Buddhist literature.

The Vulture Peak, or Griddhakuta, is traditionally associated with several teachings of the Buddha.

The First Buddhist Council is also traditionally said to have taken place at Rajagriha after the Buddha's death.

## Jain Traditions

Rajgir is also significant in Jain tradition.

The surrounding hills and landscape are associated with Mahavira and other Jain teachers.

This overlapping Buddhist and Jain heritage reflects the religious diversity of the eastern Gangetic region.

## Archaeological Remains

The remains around Rajgir include ancient fortifications, settlement remains, religious structures, and other archaeological features.

These remains provide evidence for the city's long occupation and changing political and religious importance.

## Historical Significance

Rajgir represents an important stage in the development of Magadhan political power.

Its history connects the rise of Magadha with the religious movements that emerged in the eastern Gangetic region during the first millennium BCE.
