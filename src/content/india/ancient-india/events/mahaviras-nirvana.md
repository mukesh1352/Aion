Mahavira's nirvana occupies a central place in the historical and religious memory of Jainism.

Vardhamana Mahavira is regarded by Jains as the twenty-fourth Tirthankara, the final Tirthankara of the current cosmic cycle according to Jain tradition.

## Mahavira's Life

Mahavira was born into an aristocratic family in the eastern Gangetic region.

After leaving his household life, he undertook a long period of ascetic practice.

Jain tradition describes his pursuit of liberation through discipline, meditation, nonviolence, and detachment.

## The Tirthankara

Mahavira eventually attained spiritual liberation and became a teacher of the Jain path.

His teachings emphasized ahimsa, or nonviolence, along with truth, non-stealing, celibacy, and non-possession.

He attracted a community of monks, nuns, and lay followers.

## Pava

Mahavira is traditionally said to have attained final liberation at Pava, generally identified with a location in present-day Bihar.

His nirvana became an important event in the Jain religious calendar.

## Jain Community

After Mahavira's death, his followers continued transmitting and organizing his teachings.

The Jain community developed institutional structures that allowed the tradition to survive and spread across different regions of India.

## Dating the Nirvana

Traditional Jain chronologies provide different dates for Mahavira's death.

The commonly encountered dates in historical and religious literature include 527 BCE and 468 BCE, reflecting differences between chronological traditions.

The exact date therefore remains a subject of historical discussion.

## Historical Significance

Mahavira's life and teachings became central to the development of Jainism.

The Jain tradition that emerged around his teachings became one of the enduring religious and philosophical traditions of South Asia.
