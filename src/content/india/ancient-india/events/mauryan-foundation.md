The foundation of the Mauryan Empire was a major political transformation in ancient South Asia.

Chandragupta Maurya established control over Magadha during the late fourth century BCE and began building an empire that would eventually become one of the largest political formations in ancient Indian history.

## The Political Background

The foundation of the Mauryan state followed a period of intense competition among the kingdoms and republics of northern India.

The Nanda dynasty controlled the powerful kingdom of Magadha.

The political changes associated with Alexander's campaigns in the northwest also altered the regional balance of power.

## Chandragupta Maurya

Chandragupta emerged as the founder of a new ruling dynasty.

Later traditions connect his rise with Chanakya, also known as Kautilya.

Although later literary accounts contain legendary elements, Chandragupta's establishment of Mauryan rule is supported by multiple historical traditions.

## The Fall of the Nandas

Chandragupta displaced the Nanda rulers and established himself as ruler of Magadha.

The exact sequence of events is uncertain, and later sources provide different versions of the political transition.

Nevertheless, the establishment of Mauryan control over Magadha is a central event in the history of the dynasty.

## Pataliputra

Pataliputra became the principal center of Mauryan power.

Its strategic location along the river systems of the Gangetic plain helped the new rulers govern a large and expanding territory.

## Expansion

After securing Magadha, Chandragupta expanded Mauryan authority into neighboring regions.

His later conflict and settlement with Seleucus I Nicator further strengthened Mauryan control in the northwest.

## The Beginning of an Empire

The Mauryan state developed beyond the scale of a regional Magadhan kingdom.

Its political organization, military capacity, taxation, administration, and communication networks supported rule across a large territory.

## Historical Significance

The foundation of the Mauryan Empire marked the beginning of one of the most important imperial periods in ancient South Asian history.

The state established by Chandragupta provided the political foundation for the later reigns of Bindusara and Ashoka.
