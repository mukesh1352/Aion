Muhammad of Ghor played a decisive role in the transformation of political power in northern India during the late twelfth century.

Unlike earlier Ghaznavid campaigns, Ghurid expansion created a more durable political structure in the Indo-Gangetic region.

## The Ghurid Expansion

The Ghurids originated in the mountainous region of central Afghanistan.

Muhammad gradually expanded Ghurid influence toward the Indian subcontinent.

His campaigns brought him into conflict with powerful Rajput rulers and other regional states.

## The Battles of Tarain

Muhammad fought Prithviraj Chauhan in the battles traditionally associated with Tarain.

The first major battle resulted in a Ghurid defeat.

The following confrontation ended differently, with Muhammad's forces defeating Prithviraj.

The campaigns demonstrated the importance of cavalry, military organization and strategic mobility in the politics of northern India.

## Consolidation

Military victory alone did not create the later Delhi Sultanate.

Ghurid commanders and administrators established garrisons, captured strategic centers and developed systems for maintaining political authority.

Among Muhammad's most important military commanders was Qutb al-Din Aibak.

## The Emergence of Delhi

After Muhammad's death, his territories in northern India came under the control of his former military commanders.

Qutb al-Din Aibak eventually became the first ruler of the Delhi Sultanate.

The political institutions that followed were therefore partly rooted in the Ghurid conquest.

## Historical Significance

Muhammad of Ghor is important not because he personally ruled all of northern India, but because his campaigns established the conditions for a new political order.

The Delhi Sultanate that followed would become one of the most important states in medieval Indian history.
