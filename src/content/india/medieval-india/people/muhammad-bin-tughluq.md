Muhammad bin Tughluq ruled the Delhi Sultanate from 1325 to 1351.

His reign covered an enormous political territory but was marked by repeated rebellions and difficult administrative experiments.

## An Ambitious Ruler

Muhammad was interested in philosophy, science, literature and political theory.

He also pursued ambitious plans for the administration of his empire.

Some of his policies were innovative but difficult to implement across a large and diverse territory.

## Transfer to Daulatabad

One of his most famous policies involved shifting the political center from Delhi toward Daulatabad in the Deccan.

The reasons for the move have been debated.

Strategic considerations, geographic centrality and control over the Deccan have all been proposed.

The policy caused major disruption and was eventually reversed.

## Token Currency

Muhammad also introduced token currency made from base metals.

The experiment attempted to create currency whose value depended on state authority rather than the intrinsic value of the metal.

Counterfeiting became a major problem.

The experiment failed and the currency was withdrawn.

## Taxation

Heavy taxation in parts of the empire contributed to unrest.

The Sultanate's ability to extract revenue varied considerably by region.

Administrative ambition repeatedly collided with the practical limits of governing a vast territory.

## Rebellions

During Muhammad's reign, numerous regions broke away from Delhi.

The weakening of central authority eventually contributed to the emergence of independent states.

## Historical Reputation

Medieval chroniclers often criticized Muhammad severely.

Some modern interpretations have emphasized that he was not simply irrational or incompetent, but a ruler attempting ambitious reforms under difficult political circumstances.

His reign therefore provides an important case study in the limits of state capacity.

## Significance

Muhammad bin Tughluq demonstrates the tension between imperial ambition and administrative feasibility.

His experiments remain among the most famous examples of policy innovation and failure in medieval Indian political history.
