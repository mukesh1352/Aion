Alauddin Khalji ruled the Delhi Sultanate from 1296 to 1316.

His reign represented one of the high points of Sultanate military expansion.

## Expansion

Alauddin's armies conquered or subordinated several kingdoms in northern and central India.

Military commanders such as Malik Kafur led campaigns deep into the Deccan.

These expeditions brought large amounts of wealth to Delhi and expanded the Sultanate's political influence.

## Mongol Threats

The Delhi Sultanate faced repeated Mongol incursions during Alauddin's reign.

The Mongol Empire had already transformed much of Eurasian politics.

Alauddin strengthened the military organization of the Sultanate to defend Delhi.

Several major Mongol attacks were repelled.

## Military Reforms

Alauddin introduced measures designed to maintain a large and disciplined army.

Horse branding and detailed soldier registration helped the state monitor military resources.

These policies were connected to the broader effort to prevent corruption and maintain military readiness.

## Market Regulation

Alauddin became famous for regulating markets in Delhi.

Prices of grain, cloth, horses and other goods were monitored.

The state attempted to ensure that soldiers could be maintained without requiring extremely high salaries.

The system depended on strong administrative supervision.

## Deccan Campaigns

The campaigns led by Malik Kafur brought several southern kingdoms into a tributary relationship with Delhi.

The Sultanate's armies reached territories far beyond the traditional northern core.

Permanent control over all these regions, however, was difficult to maintain.

## Historical Significance

Alauddin Khalji's reign demonstrates the military and administrative capacity of the Delhi Sultanate at its strongest.

His policies also show how medieval states could intervene deeply in markets, taxation and military organization.
