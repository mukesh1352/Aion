# Panini

Panini was an ancient Sanskrit grammarian, usually placed around the fifth or fourth century BCE. His *Ashtadhyayi* became the foundational grammatical description of Classical Sanskrit.

## Why they matter

Panini's rule system is one of the most sophisticated surviving works of ancient linguistic analysis. It describes morphology, syntax, phonology, and word formation through tightly organized rules and technical conventions.

## Historical evidence

The principal evidence is the *Ashtadhyayi* itself, a work of roughly four thousand sutras. Its language and references provide evidence about Sanskrit and aspects of the social and geographic world known to its author.

Later grammarians, especially Katyayana and Patanjali, quote, comment on, and preserve the Paninian grammatical tradition. This later transmission is one reason Panini's authorship and influence are unusually well documented for an ancient Indian scholar.

## Traditions and later accounts

Later biographical traditions place Panini in particular regions and connect him with legendary episodes. These stories are not necessary to establish his historical importance and should not be treated as contemporary biography.

His exact birthplace and dates remain approximate rather than precisely established.
