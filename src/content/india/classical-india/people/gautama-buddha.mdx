# Gautama Buddha

Gautama Buddha was a historical religious teacher whose community and teachings became the foundation of Buddhism. Most current scholarship places his life broadly in the fifth century BCE, but there is no scholarly consensus on exact birth and death dates.

## Why they matter

The Buddha was central to the formation of one of South Asia's major intellectual and religious traditions. His teachings on suffering, causation, ethics, meditation, and liberation became the basis for Buddhist communities across Asia.

## Historical evidence

There is no surviving document written during the Buddha's lifetime. The earliest substantial evidence comes from Buddhist textual traditions transmitted orally before being written down and from later inscriptions.

Ashoka's third-century BCE inscriptions provide important early evidence for an established Buddhist community and specifically commemorate Lumbini as the Buddha's birthplace. Early Buddhist textual traditions preserve remembered discourses, monastic rules, and accounts of the Buddha's community, but they were compiled and transmitted over time.

## Traditions and later accounts

Buddhist biographies contain stories of royal patronage, miracles, previous lives, the Buddha's family, and the circumstances of his enlightenment. These are important religious traditions, but they should not be treated as independently established historical events.

The traditional dates 563–483 BCE are still widely encountered, while many historians favor a later fifth-century BCE chronology.
