# Charaka

Charaka is the physician traditionally associated with the *Charaka Samhita*, one of the foundational surviving works of classical Indian medicine. The historical person and the layers of the text must be distinguished.

## Why they matter

The Charaka tradition is central to the history of Ayurveda. The text discusses diagnosis, physiology, therapeutics, diet, medical training, professional ethics, and the relationship between physician, patient, and attendants.

## Historical evidence

The *Charaka Samhita* survives as a Sanskrit medical compendium based on an earlier tradition associated with Agnivesha. Its present form contains layers of compilation and revision, and the text was later revised by Drdhabala.

This means the medical ideas can be studied historically without assuming that every section was written by a single physician named Charaka.

## Traditions and later accounts

Ayurvedic tradition presents Charaka as an eminent physician and author. Later stories may connect him with royal courts or legendary circumstances, but these details are not securely established by contemporary evidence.

The commonly cited first- or second-century CE date is a broad scholarly placement for the Charaka textual tradition, not a securely documented lifespan.
