# Kalidasa

Kalidasa is the most celebrated poet and dramatist of classical Sanskrit literature. His surviving works include *Abhijnanashakuntalam*, *Meghaduta*, and *Raghuvamsha*.

## Why they matter

Kalidasa's poetry and drama shaped Sanskrit literary aesthetics for centuries. His treatment of nature, love, kingship, landscape, and moral choice became models for later Indian literature.

## Historical evidence

The works attributed to Kalidasa survive in manuscript traditions and are studied through their language, style, intertextual relationships, and transmission history. Their literary features are broadly consistent with a Gupta-period setting, and many scholars place Kalidasa around the fifth century CE.

What is missing is a secure contemporary biography. There is no known inscription from Kalidasa himself that establishes his court, birthplace, or exact dates.

## Traditions and later accounts

Later stories portray Kalidasa as a court poet of Vikramaditya, often identified with Chandragupta II, and include elaborate tales about his education and miraculous rise. These stories are important to Kalidasa's cultural memory but are not secure historical biography.

The famous Navaratna group is a later literary tradition and should not be treated as a contemporary court roster.
