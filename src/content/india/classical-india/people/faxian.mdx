# Faxian

Faxian was a Chinese Buddhist monk who travelled from China through Central Asia to India and Sri Lanka between approximately 399 and 414 CE. He is included here as an external observer rather than as an Indian historical figure.

## Why they matter

Faxian's travel account is one of the most valuable surviving external descriptions of India during the Gupta period. It records Buddhist monasteries and sacred sites while also commenting on travel, administration, charity, social groups, and everyday conditions.

## Historical evidence

Faxian's own travel narrative, conventionally known as the *Record of Buddhist Kingdoms*, is the principal evidence. He describes places associated with the Buddha, Buddhist institutions, routes, and practices that he encountered.

His observations are geographically selective and shaped by his religious purpose. They therefore provide evidence for particular communities and places rather than a comprehensive portrait of all Indian society.

## Traditions and later accounts

Later retellings sometimes use Faxian as if he were an impartial census-taker of Gupta India. His account is better understood as a pilgrim's selective observation. His descriptions of prosperity, law, charity, and social exclusion should be read alongside Indian inscriptions, archaeology, and other literary sources.
