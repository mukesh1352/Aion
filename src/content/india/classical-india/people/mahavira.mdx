# Mahavira

Mahavira, also known as Vardhamana, was a major teacher of the Jain tradition and the figure remembered in Jainism as its twenty-fourth Tirthankara. His historical existence is accepted, while the chronology of his life remains disputed.

## Why they matter

Mahavira helped shape one of ancient India's major Shramana traditions, emphasizing non-violence, ascetic discipline, restraint, and liberation from rebirth. Jain communities and institutions that developed around his teaching became enduring parts of South Asian religious history.

## Historical evidence

Early Jain canonical traditions preserve teachings, monastic rules, genealogies, and narratives associated with Mahavira. Buddhist literature also contains references to Jain ascetics and to a contemporary teacher identified by later scholarship with Mahavira.

The surviving evidence is not contemporary biography in the modern sense. Dating relies on layered textual traditions and cross-comparison with the chronology of other fifth-century BCE figures.

## Traditions and later accounts

Śvetāmbara and Digambara traditions preserve different chronological schemes. The traditional Śvetāmbara dates are 599–527 BCE, while Digambara chronology gives a different death date. Modern scholarship commonly places Mahavira later, broadly in the fifth century BCE.

Stories of his previous births, supernatural events, and detailed childhood episodes belong to Jain religious literature and should be distinguished from historically secure evidence.
