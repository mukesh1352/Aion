# Patanjali

Patanjali is the name traditionally attached to the *Mahabhashya*, a major second-century BCE commentary on Sanskrit grammar. The grammarian is historically important, but the identification of this Patanjali with the author of the *Yoga Sutras* is not established.

## Why they matter

The *Mahabhashya* preserves a mature stage of the Paninian grammatical tradition and provides evidence about language, teaching, debate, and aspects of society in ancient India.

## Historical evidence

The *Mahabhashya* is the central source. It comments on selected rules of Panini and on material attributed to Katyayana. Its linguistic and historical references support a date around the second century BCE, commonly around 150 BCE.

The work also contains examples and observations that historians use cautiously to reconstruct the intellectual and social environment of its period.

## Traditions and later accounts

A separate Sanskrit work, the *Yoga Sutras*, is traditionally attributed to Patanjali. Modern scholarship does not regard the identification of the grammarian and the yoga author as certain. Later biographies and hagiographies also add details that cannot be independently verified.
