# Samudragupta

Samudragupta was a fourth-century Gupta ruler whose reign expanded Gupta influence across much of northern India and into the wider political networks of the subcontinent.

## Why they matter

Samudragupta's reign is one of the best-documented episodes of Gupta imperial expansion. His political program combined direct conquest, subordinate rulers, tribute relationships, and diplomacy.

## Historical evidence

The Allahabad Pillar Prashasti, composed by the court poet and minister Harishena, is the principal narrative source. It records campaigns, relationships with neighboring rulers, and the king's royal ideology.

Gupta gold coins provide additional evidence for Samudragupta's titulature, imagery, political claims, and monetary economy. The inscription is a royal panegyric, so its claims must be read as political representation rather than neutral reporting.

## Traditions and later accounts

Later historians sometimes describe Samudragupta as a uniformly conquering emperor. The inscription itself presents a more differentiated pattern of conquest, submission, reinstatement, tribute, and frontier relationships.

Claims of a completely unified subcontinent under Samudragupta go beyond what the evidence supports.
