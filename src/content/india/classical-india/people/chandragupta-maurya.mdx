# Chandragupta Maurya

Chandragupta Maurya was the founder of the Mauryan dynasty and the ruler who transformed Magadha into the core of a large imperial state in the late fourth century BCE. His reign is reconstructed from Greek accounts, later Indian traditions, and the political landscape reflected in Mauryan sources.

## Why they matter

Chandragupta marks a major transition from competing mahajanapadas to large-scale imperial rule. His conflict and eventual settlement with Seleucus I connected the Mauryan state directly with the Hellenistic kingdoms of western Asia.

## Historical evidence

The strongest external evidence comes from Greek and Roman writers preserving material ultimately derived from the Hellenistic world, especially accounts associated with Megasthenes. These sources describe the Mauryan court and administration, although they survive only in later quotations and fragments.

The Mauryan state is also securely established by archaeology and, more directly for the dynasty's later reign, Ashokan inscriptions. Chandragupta's treaty with Seleucus is historically plausible and widely accepted, but the surviving evidence does not preserve a full contemporary narrative of the war or settlement.

## Traditions and later accounts

Buddhist, Jain, and Sanskrit traditions provide detailed stories about Chandragupta's origins, rise, advisers, conversion, and final years. These traditions are important for the history of memory surrounding the Mauryan founder, but individual episodes cannot all be treated as contemporary fact.

The famous partnership with Chanakya is supported by later literary tradition; the exact historical relationship between the two cannot be reconstructed with certainty from contemporary evidence.
