# Aryabhata

Aryabhata was a mathematician and astronomer active in the late fifth and early sixth centuries CE. His *Aryabhatiya*, completed when he was 23, is one of the most important surviving scientific works of classical India.

## Why they matter

Aryabhata developed computational methods for arithmetic and algebra, gave systematic treatments of trigonometric quantities, and presented an astronomical model in which the apparent daily motion of the sky is explained through Earth's rotation.

## Historical evidence

The *Aryabhatiya* is unusually valuable because it names its author and contains an internal chronological statement. The work is conventionally dated to 499 CE, when Aryabhata was 23, leading to the widely used birth date of 476 CE.

His verses discuss planetary periods, eclipses, place-value computation, and mathematical procedures. Later scholars such as Bhaskara I preserved and commented on his work.

## Traditions and later accounts

Later biographies sometimes attach Aryabhata to particular cities or royal courts. The identification of his birthplace and the exact circumstances of his career are less secure than the evidence for his work.

His astronomical model should be described in its own historical context rather than treated as an anticipation of all later heliocentric astronomy.
