# Prabhavati Gupta

Prabhavati Gupta was a daughter of Chandragupta II of the Gupta dynasty and queen of the Vakataka ruler Rudrasena II. After her husband's death, she exercised authority as regent while her sons were still young.

## Why they matter

Prabhavati Gupta is one of the clearest epigraphic examples of a woman exercising dynastic and political authority in classical India. Her inscriptions also illuminate marriage alliances, land grants, religious patronage, and the interaction between Gupta and Vakataka power.

## Historical evidence

Her copper-plate grants are the principal evidence. The records identify her Gupta lineage, her marriage into the Vakataka dynasty, and her role in issuing grants during the minority of her sons.

The inscriptions are especially valuable because they are contemporary records rather than later literary biographies.

## Traditions and later accounts

Later historical narratives sometimes simplify Prabhavati's role into a purely dynastic marriage. The inscriptions instead show a ruler or regent issuing formal grants and presenting herself through both Gupta and Vakataka genealogical identities.

Her exact birth and death dates are not securely recorded; the dates used here are an approximate scholarly placement for her period of activity.
