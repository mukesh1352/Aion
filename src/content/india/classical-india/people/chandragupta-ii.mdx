# Chandragupta II

Chandragupta II, also known by the title Vikramaditya in Gupta records and later tradition, ruled during a major phase of Gupta expansion and cultural production.

## Why they matter

His reign extended Gupta influence toward western India and strengthened access to commercial networks connected with the Arabian Sea. The period is also associated with major developments in Sanskrit literature, sculpture, coinage, and religious institutions.

## Historical evidence

Large quantities of Gupta coinage portray Chandragupta II and provide secure evidence for his rule, titulature, imagery, and monetary system. Inscriptions from the Gupta period also document his dynasty and political environment.

The Mehrauli Iron Pillar inscription is often connected with a Gupta king named Chandra and has traditionally been identified with Chandragupta II, but the identification is not completely certain.

## Traditions and later accounts

The name Vikramaditya became the focus of a large later cycle of stories about an ideal king, including the Navaratna tradition. These later narratives should not be projected backward as straightforward biography.

The association of Kalidasa and other celebrated figures with Chandragupta II is plausible within Gupta-period cultural history but cannot be demonstrated in every case as direct court patronage.
