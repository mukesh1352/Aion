# Ashoka's Accession

Ashoka's accession is reconstructed from later Buddhist chronologies and the chronology of his inscriptions. The precise date is conventionally placed around 268 BCE, but succession details are less secure than the evidence for his later reign. His accession created the context for one of the richest bodies of royal epigraphy in ancient South Asia.

## Background

This event belongs to a period of major political, religious, economic or intellectual change. The surrounding chronology matters because Classical India was not governed by a single continuous imperial structure.

## What happened

The event developed through interactions among rulers, communities, institutions and geographic networks. Surviving evidence usually preserves only selected aspects of the process, so a chronological reconstruction should distinguish what a source states directly from what historians infer.

## Historical evidence

Historians reconstruct the event from a combination of inscriptions, coins, archaeological remains, literary sources and foreign accounts. Each source has its own purpose and limitations.

## Historical debate

Important questions remain open where evidence is fragmentary. Royal inscriptions may exaggerate victories; religious narratives may preserve later traditions; archaeological evidence may demonstrate settlement without identifying the actors involved.
